The Meridian

Rain hammered the windows of Hale House on the night of Lady Margaret Hale's birthday dinner. At twenty to ten the lights went out across the whole manor. When they came back on ten minutes later, the study safe stood open and the Meridian was gone. The Meridian was a sapphire pendant, the last gift from Margaret's late husband, Edmund. The storm had flooded the bridge road, so no one could leave before morning. Whoever had taken the pendant was still inside the house.

Detective Rowe arrived with Officer Pip Tanner and went first to the study. The safe had no scratches or drill marks, so whoever opened it had known the combination. In the pocket of a coat hanging nearby, Rowe found a letter addressed to Victor Crane, Edmund's former business partner. It demanded two thousand pounds and was signed only with the letter "M." Down in the cellar, Rowe found that no fuse had blown. Someone had pulled the main lever by hand and left a fresh smudge of black grease on the handle. The blackout had been no accident.

Lady Margaret said she was the only one who knew the combination: one-four-zero-three, the fourteenth of March, the day Edmund died. Rowe pointed out that others might remember a date like that. Margaret admitted that Victor would, and so would Dr. Julian Ash, the family doctor who had been at Edmund's side at the end. She also recalled that both men had left the dinner table just before the lights went out.

Victor insisted he had spent the whole blackout on the telephone in the hall. He admitted he owed money to a man he would not name. Officer Tanner checked with the telephone exchange, and the operator confirmed that Victor had been on a call from 9:38 to 9:52. He had never left the hall.

Elsie Moore, the maid, had been sent to fetch candles from the pantry, and her apron was still spotted with fresh wax. She had passed the back stairs by the study in the dark and noticed a sharp smell of carbolic, like a hospital. She was also the one who found the cellar lever pulled down and pushed it back up.

Dr. Ash said he had stepped out to fetch a tonic from his medical bag. When Rowe asked about Edmund, he said he had signed the death certificate himself and would never forget the date, the fourteenth of March. As he spoke, his shirt cuff slid back to show a black smear of grease. He blamed a carriage wheel. When Rowe asked to look inside his bag, he refused and held it a little too tightly.

Rowe gathered everyone in the drawing room and named Dr. Ash as the thief. Ash had left the table and pulled the cellar lever, and the grease was still on his cuff. He had climbed the back stairs carrying the smell of carbolic, and he had opened the safe with the date he had written on Edmund's death certificate. Ash broke down and confessed that he owed money to the same man Victor did, the man known only as "M." Officer Tanner opened the medical bag and found the Meridian hidden beneath a false bottom.

The case was closed, but one question remained: who is "M"?
